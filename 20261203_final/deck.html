Frostroot: The Ramble

23 January 2016, the night of the record blizzard. Two people are lost in the Ramble; something made of roots is hunting them. Design the park for the storm: who gets out?

Ryan Fu · Lu Liu · Yifan Zhao — William Martin

The simulation

Real terrain and 2,427 trees from NYC LiDAR, paths from OpenStreetMap, the hourly weather of that night from the Central Park station. Fog is the observed visibility; warm light is safety.

The semantic model, in one slide

  • Place: 3 m grid of the Ramble (244 × 215 cells), ground cover, LiDAR terrain and trees as solids, a 406-node path graph, 42 named places.
  • Time: the storm clock of 23–24 Jan 2016, hourly observations; snow and drift from them; tracks fill in.
  • Location: W (metres), P (on a path), N (named place), E (egocentric), and each agent's landmark chain.
  • Distance: Euclidean, acoustic (muffled, wind), travel time (Tobler, snow, per kind), perceptual (visibility fingerprints).
  • Agents: survivors and the creature, each with its own map, vision and hearing; plans on what it knows.
  • Design: plowed / closed paths, lamps, shelters, signs within a budget; scored on 24 rounds with common random numbers; manual and (1+1)-ES automatic modes.
